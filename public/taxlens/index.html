Peer groups — K-Means

Grouped on seven income-source shares plus scale. Deductions are deliberately kept out, so high claims cannot become "normal" by construction.

STAGE 2 · Isolation Forest

Segmentation changes who is reviewed

Input: 55 features · 8 groupsOutput: anomaly score and 2% flag per return

One 200-tree forest per group scores each return by how few random splits separate it from its peers — a landlord is compared with landlords, not with everyone.

STAGE 3 · Supervised machine learning

The flag rule is learnable

Input: 55 features + Isolation Forest flag as labelOutput: precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC

Random forest and histogram gradient boosting are trained to reproduce the Stage 2 flag on a hold-out set of 96,567 returns (1,933 flagged, ≈ 2% positives). Accuracy is 0.98 for both — and for predicting "normal" for everyone — so it is not used.

A flag is a statistical peer-group signal only — never evidence of non-compliance or fraud. Amounts are for the 2022–23 income year; fields left blank count as $0. Investment income up to $10,000 is treated as interest and half of anything above that as capital gains (with the 50% discount); employer super is assumed at 10.5% of salary; the totals are derived automatically.

A flag is

  • A statistical anomaly relative to a peer group
  • At most, a potential compliance-risk indicator
  • A ranked, explained starting point for human review

A flag is not

  • Evidence of fraud or non-compliance
  • A finding — false positives dominate by design
  • Stable across segmentations: boundary taxpayers can change status

What the evidence does not cover

No audit labelsOnly statistical anomaly is measured; sensitivity to real non-compliance is unknown.
One year, 2% sampleUnusual in one year may be a normal transition. The upper tail is compressed.
Entity-spanning behaviourArrangements across companies, trusts, years or families are invisible to 55 features.
SHAP explains the modelNot the data-generating process; attributions depend on the feature set.
